Why Glen?

Every agent gives the same answer

When every person and every agent reads and writes one source of truth, they stop drifting into separate, contradictory versions of it. Different teams answer from the same facts, and each answer carries the turn it came from, so anyone can check why it is what it is.

In practice

Two teams ask their agents about the refund policy and get the same answer, traced to the decision that set it, not three stale copies that quietly disagree.

New people are productive on day one

When someone joins, they and their agents inherit a living snapshot of how the org actually works: the decisions, the conventions, and the reasons behind them. Ramp-up collapses from months to the first session, and anyone can do anyone's job to the same standard.

In practice

A new engineer's first pull request already follows your patterns. Their agent read the architectural decisions and the gotchas the team wrote months ago.

When people leave, the knowledge stays

Every decision someone made, every hard-won lesson, every customer quirk they learned stays with the org instead of in one person's head. When they move on, none of it walks out the door. Turnover stops costing you context.

In practice

A staff engineer resigns on Friday. On Monday, their replacement's agent still knows why the last migration happened and what broke the time before.

Your best people become everyone's coach

The judgment of your top performers becomes shared, replayable training. New hires can review calls and decisions as if they were sitting with the person who does it best, and their agents inherit that standard instead of generic advice.

In practice

A new rep runs call review as Thomas, your head of sales. The agent objects, paces, and closes the way Thomas does, because it learned from his calls.

Every account, understood over time

Glen keeps one growing record of each customer. Every call, ticket, and piece of work teaches the org that account's history and preferences, so the context never resets to zero when a different person or agent picks it up.

In practice

A teammate covers an account they have never touched. Their agent already knows the renewal date, the integration that broke last quarter, and who prefers email over calls.

Agents inherit how your experts use tools

Your agents act through tools over MCP, and they inherit how your people drive them. The hours one person spends teaching an agent a tool become every agent's starting point, so what the org learns turns into action, not recall alone.

In practice

Someone spends ten hours mastering Ashby with their agent. The next agent to touch Ashby already knows your stages, your fields, and your conventions.

Every decision, on the record for good

Glen keeps when decisions were made, who made them, and what led to them: one queryable timeline of the whole organization. Run a postmortem and walk the exact chain of choices that produced an outcome. The record still holds up ten or fifteen years from now.

In practice

Tracing a Q2 outage, you follow the thread back to a January design call, the trade-off noted in March, and who signed off, in minutes instead of weeks.
