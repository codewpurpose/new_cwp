Every agent learns as one

Coding agents, support bots, the cron job on a runner: every agent in the company writes into one store. What any one of them learns, the rest recall.

Anyone can work like anyone

Your platform engineer's deploy recipe, your support lead's escalation playbook. Once one agent has seen it done right, every agent can do it that way, including the new hire's.

Context windows close and sessions end; the store keeps everything. A decision recorded in March is still recallable next March, reasoning included.

Your best people become everyone's coach

The judgment of your top performers becomes shared, replayable training. New hires can review calls and decisions as if they were sitting with the person who does it best, and their agents inherit that standard instead of generic advice.

In practice

A new rep runs call review as Thomas, your head of sales. The agent objects, paces, and closes the way Thomas does, because it learned from his calls.

Every account, understood over time

Glen keeps one growing record of each customer. Every call, ticket, and piece of work teaches the org that account's history and preferences, so the context never resets to zero when a different person or agent picks it up.

In practice

A teammate covers an account they have never touched. Their agent already knows the renewal date, the integration that broke last quarter, and who prefers email over calls.

Agents inherit how your experts use tools

Your agents act through tools over MCP, and they inherit how your people drive them. The hours one person spends teaching an agent a tool become every agent's starting point, so what the org learns turns into action, not recall alone.

In practice

Someone spends ten hours mastering Ashby with their agent. The next agent to touch Ashby already knows your stages, your fields, and your conventions.

Every decision, on the record for good

Glen keeps when decisions were made, who made them, and what led to them: one queryable timeline of the whole organization. Run a postmortem and walk the exact chain of choices that produced an outcome. The record still holds up ten or fifteen years from now.

In practice

Tracing a Q2 outage, you follow the thread back to a January design call, the trade-off noted in March, and who signed off, in minutes instead of weeks.

Glen is shared learning for AI agents. Your agents call one tool each turn to get the facts that matter and store anything new. What any of them learns belongs to your whole org, so no agent ever starts from scratch.

A vector database is a box you have to fill, tune, and query yourself. Glen is the finished service: it decides what to store and what's relevant for you, with no index to manage or retrieval code to write. And because it's shared across your org, knowledge compounds instead of being stuck in one app.

Everything Glen learns is shared across everyone in your organization, and that's what lets it compound. It's isolated at the org boundary: one organization's records can never be read by another, enforced with row-level security in the database. For anything too sensitive to share, switch on private mode and nothing from that chat is ever written.

Anything that speaks MCP: Claude and Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, and any custom agent on an MCP client. The same configuration works everywhere, so connecting one agent connects them all.

Your data lives in Postgres, encrypted in transit with TLS and at rest by the cloud provider. API keys are hashed with argon2id and all agent access goes through OAuth 2.1, so there are no long-lived secrets in agent configs.
